The cases that matter

Standard AI is built for the common case. We build for the rest.

The findings that change a patient’s outcome are often the ones a trained model is least prepared for.

01

Early findings too subtle to see

A tumor at the stage where treatment works best can look almost like healthy tissue. Models trained on obvious examples learn to wait for obvious examples.

What we do. We evolve the model against confirmed early cases, with a hard floor on recall for the finding that matters, so it learns to commit before the finding is large.

02

Rare disease with almost no data

For most rare conditions no single hospital has enough examples to train on. A model that needs thousands of cases never gets built.

What we do. We start from systems that already read anatomy well and evolve how they augment, weight, and vote on the few cases that exist.

03

The scan looks different at your hospital

Different scanners, protocols, and patient populations change what a model sees. Accuracy measured somewhere else does not travel.

What we do. Every candidate is scored on your scanners and your sites, and the one that is worst on any single site is the one we judge it by.

04

The patients the training data left out

A model that is accurate on average can be unreliable for a group that was underrepresented when it was trained.

What we do. The gap between groups is one of the objectives the model is evolved on. It cannot win by getting better for some patients at the expense of others.

Why evolution and not more tuning

The settings inside a medical model interact. The right decision threshold depends on the loss weighting, which depends on the preprocessing. Tuning them one at a time stops at the first local optimum. A population explores combinations, and crossover between strong candidates carries working combinations forward together. In genetics this interaction is called epistasis. It is the reason we search this way.

What the model is judged on

  • Recall on the finding that matters, at the false positive budget your readers can live with
  • Accuracy on the worst scanner and site, not the average
  • The gap between patient groups
  • Whether a 90 percent score means the same thing on every case
  • Inference time inside the reading workflow

No single number. The output is a set of models with different tradeoffs, and your clinical team picks the operating point.
